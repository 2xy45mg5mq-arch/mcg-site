Imposter Syndrome

written by
Max Coyne-Green
In a city where everyone is faking it, an uptight tech founder and
his broke actor roommate accidentally switch bodies –
and finally get their lives together.

Gus and Wally share a SoHo loft, but nothing in common.

Gus, a former tech wiz kid who sold his company at 19, owns the place. He grinds 80-hours a week in a big bedroom upstairs, betting everything on his new startup. His only real friend is his turtle, Snacks.

Wally, a hot broke actor, isn't on his level — literally. For a fraction of the rent, he stays downstairs in a curtained-off corner of the living room. Still, he seems to be living a lot better. Every weekend, he has big parties on their roof deck while Gus is working inside.

Gus would never admit it, but he's jealous of Wally's chill lifestyle and the attention he gets from women.

And Wally, bartending as he struggles to book gigs, secretly wishes he had Gus's stability and future.

One night, Gus is working on a pitch for bigshot VC investor. But Wally, like usual, is having a party right outside his room. When the party spills into Gus's room and his turtle Snacks goes missing, Gus reaches his limit and tears into Wally — calling him an immature loser who's ruining his future. Wally says Gus is an uptight loner wasting his youth. He grabs the rescued turtle and holds him overhead, like a playground bully, refusing to hand him over until Gus admits he needs him. Snacks, overwhelmed, bites them both.

They get dizzy and pass out.

The next morning, the two of them wake up. As each other.

Desperate for answers, they dig into ChatGPT. But it recommends seeking psychological help.

With Gus's VC pitch and Wally's audition coming up, they have no choice but to do their best as the other. It's a major risk. But their biggest flaws surprisingly blow open the opportunities.

Relaxed, confident, and hesitant to share details, the New Gus attracts excitement from the investors.

Meanwhile, the New Wally works harder than the original Wally ever did, landing a dream movie role.

Soon enough, they're becoming the person the other was supposed to be.

But you can't fake it forever. Unless you make it forever.
